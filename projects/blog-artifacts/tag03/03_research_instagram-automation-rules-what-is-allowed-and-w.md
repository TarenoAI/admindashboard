# Research — Agent 1

## Topic
**Instagram Automation Rules: What Is Allowed and What Gets You Restricted**

## Search Intent
- Understand safe vs risky automation behavior on Instagram
- Get practical execution rules for creators, freelancers, and small teams
- Avoid account restrictions while keeping consistent publishing workflows

## Audience Assumptions
- Audience runs Instagram for business outcomes, not vanity posting
- They use at least one scheduling or workflow tool
- They want clear boundaries, not legal theory
- They need repeatable rules for team handoffs

## Competitor Patterns (Top 6)
1. Generic “dos and don’ts” lists without operational steps
2. Heavy focus on fear (“ban risk”) without governance workflow
3. Weak distinction between publishing automation and engagement automation
4. Missing escalation logic for high-risk actions
5. Low clarity on role ownership in teams
6. No weekly review framework for ongoing compliance

## Competitor Gaps (Top 6)
1. No decision matrix for allowed / caution / forbidden actions
2. No practical “when to automate, when not to automate” criteria
3. No audit checklist for account safety
4. No concrete handling of volatile claims in policy content
5. No implementation blueprint for solo vs team operations
6. No structured failure-pattern section

## Differentiation Angle
Position automation as an **operating discipline**: automate publishing and workflow visibility, never fake engagement behavior. Add a governance layer (ownership + escalation + weekly review).

## Risky / Volatile Claim Zones
- “Safe”, “guaranteed”, “ban-proof” language
- Exact percentages for restriction likelihood
- Exact platform thresholds without primary sources
- Time-bound policy claims that can change quickly

## Claim Safety Direction
- Use conservative wording: “lower risk”, “can reduce”, “commonly restricted”
- Avoid exact numbers unless source-backed
- Keep policy statements principle-based and operational
